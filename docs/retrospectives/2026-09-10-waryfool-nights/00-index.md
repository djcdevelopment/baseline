# Waryfool Nights

_An account of one long session on the mechnet, 9th–10th September 2026._

Written to be read aloud. Ten chapters, roughly thirty-five minutes.

## What this is

A photography campaign inside a video game archive. Three machines, one Steam
account, twelve thousand pictures of things strangers built, and about four hours
lost to a bug that was never a bug.

It is told honestly, which means the agent in it is wrong a lot, and the human in
it is right in ways he could not immediately prove. That is the interesting part.
Anyone can narrate a success.

## The cast

**Derek** — the operator. Runs the fleet. Down to one percent of his weekly budget
when the story opens, which turns out to matter.

**Claude** — the agent. Has tools, timestamps, and a great deal of confidence.
Reads log files in the wrong order.

**OMEN** — the workstation. Two Arc Pro B70s, a pinned copy of Valheim, and the
archive drive. Does most of the work in the end.

**AM4** — the capture node. Ubuntu, thirty gigs of RAM, an RTX 5070 with no
inference engine bound to it, and a wireless link everyone blames for everything.

**i5** — a laptop. Present mostly as a cautionary tale.

**FX99** — the monitoring node. Quietly turns out to be the public origin for
everything, which nobody had written down.

## A note on the numbers

Every figure spoken in these chapters came off a live system: a status file, a
Steam connection log, a systemd journal, a hash. Where the account says a thing
was verified, it was verified. Where it says the agent believed something, the
agent merely believed it. The difference is the whole point.
