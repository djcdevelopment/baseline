# Guild Master Interview Guide

**For the interviewer.** This is a ~20-minute, conversational interview. The GM
has poured their heart into this community. **This is an invitation and a
recognition of their expertise — never an audit, never a verdict on how they've
run things.** We're here to learn their world so we can make it *cheaper to
care*. Keep it warm, keep it in their vocabulary, remind them they're in control.
There are no wrong answers.

---

### Intro (read/adapt)
> "Thanks for taking 20 minutes. You've built something real here, and I want to
> be clear up front: this isn't an audit, and there are no wrong answers. I just
> want to learn how your guild actually works so we can build something that
> makes the repetitive stuff easier — completely under your control — and give
> you back time to actually play the community you built."

### A. How events actually happen (run-log reality)
1. **Spotting the farmers.** When it comes to your event rules, how do you
   usually spot someone taking advantage? Are you mostly checking hard limits
   (like "only twice a week"), or are there softer signs of abuse you or your
   team just have to catch by eye?
2. **Keeping the records.** How do you track event records right now? Does the
   creator/host log the results, or do players submit them (like a turn-in)? Do
   you note if it was done solo, and do you track who ended up with the loot?
3. **Clarifying the terms.** When your rules talk about someone "running an
   event," does that usually mean they *hosted* it for others, or that they
   *played and completed* it?

### B. Toil inventory
4. **The grind.** What's the most repetitive, tedious thing you do for the guild
   most weeks that you wish you didn't have to?
5. **The return.** If a magic wand removed that task and gave you those hours
   back, what would you spend the time on in the community instead?

### C. Vocabulary
6. **Your language.** Every guild has its own flavor. What are your specific
   names for your ranks, your points/currency, your events, and your player
   submissions? (Any tool should render back in *your* words, never flatten them
   into a generic template.)

### D. Feel
7. **Trust.** What's the one thing a tool like this should do — or absolutely
   *never* do — to make you feel it's entirely yours and worth trusting?
