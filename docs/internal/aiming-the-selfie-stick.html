Aiming the selfie stick

There is a literature for this, and it turns out to be four separate ones that rarely cite each other. Here is what they name, what 2,509 of our own frames say when you measure instead of assume, and the shots worth taking next.

The short version

The rig already does the one convention that matters most — the 45° corner shot. Of everything else the catalogs offer, the biggest untried wins are standing at the foot of a tower and looking up, and a plan view of the sprawling districts. Both are one parameter away.

And a warning that runs through all of it: our aesthetic scorer cannot judge any of this. It reads brightness. Ask it about composition and it answers about exposure.

Four literatures, not one

They describe the same act and share almost no vocabulary.

Aerial photogrammetry — the one with actual numbers

This is the body of work your sky-platform method landed in, and it defines its terms in degrees rather than adjectives:

TermTilt from verticalWhat it shows
Nadir0° ±5Straight down. Pure plan.

Low oblique~30°Ground surface, no horizon
Typical oblique40–50°The working default
High oblique60°+Horizon enters the frame

You derived a variant of a named convention from first principles, and you were right to go steeper: a sky platform has five kilometres of nothing beneath it, so the terrain a low oblique exists to include is not available at any distance inside the haze cap. Measured, that method returned 0% occluded and luma 55.5 against a gallery median of 96 — where 65° overhead had come back 100% occluded and 22° side-on 76%.

Architectural — and we already do its main trick

The three-quarter or angular view sits at roughly 45° to the subject so the camera catches two faces at once, reading volume and proportion in a single frame. It carries most hero shots of exteriors. orbit_azimuths() already offsets 45° from each structure's long axis, so this convention is built in — worth knowing before anyone proposes it as an improvement.

Landscape — layering and leading lines

Foreground, midground, background, with something in each and a line that walks the eye between them. depth_layers.py already measures this per frame. We have never planned for it.

Cinematography — the big glossary

Fifty-plus named angles and shot sizes. Most are about a moving subject and do not transfer, but the establishing shot does, and we have no equivalent: every frame we take is framed to fill.

One correction worth carrying

Lens compression is a myth. Perspective is determined by camera position and nothing else; focal length only changes the crop. Long lenses look "compressed" because photographers stand further back to use them.

So the rig already controls the only lever that matters, and the consequence is better than it first looks: if focal length only crops, the crop can happen after the shutter. At 4K into a 1,600 px render there is a 2.2× focal length sitting unused in every frame we have ever taken, and a field-of-view setting in the mod would buy nothing we cannot already do in post. See paired standoff below.

It reads global tone and nothing else — an exposure meter and a veto, not a critic. Use it to kill black frames and fog whiteouts and to choose time and weather. Never use it to judge a framing change.

The sun, measured from the frames

No game access needed. The photographs know where it was.

Regress sky-strip luminance — the top 18% of the frame — against camera yaw, differenced within each build so the subject cancels out. Whole-frame luminance does not work: R² ≈ 0, because the building dominates. The strip does. Seven independent runs at time 0.64:

092513 260°125325 243°145411 216° 165629 237°180143 207°220312 222° 083226 251°

Pooled: ~235°, south-west, ±25°. Good enough to place a back-lit camera, not good enough for a precise sun-behind-the-ridge shot.

Its first use was a null result. Labelling all 1,087 golden frames by lighting direction gives back-lit 5.599, side-lit 5.628, front-lit 5.610 — r = −0.003. Either direction genuinely does not matter in Valheim's soft golden light, or the scorer cannot see it. Given everything above, the second is far more likely.

The verdict on light

Eight builds, identical camera, golden 0.64 against twilight 0.71.

Daylight leads. It rakes shadows across terrain, puts white caps on the water, and makes build materials legible — which for a gallery of what people built is close to the whole point. Golden's best frame beat twilight's on 29 of 30 builds (medians 5.911 against 5.611) — though remember the scorer is biased toward the brighter frame, so that margin is not evidence, only agreement.

Twilight keeps a place anyway, for a reason that is about the builders rather than the camera: it is the only light that shows their lighting. Braziers, candles and groundtorches placed so a base is comfortable to be in after dark are a deliberate act, and daylight hides it completely.

So golden leads and twilight is the second frame on builds that earn it. No special-casing needed — the scorer already sorts golden first, for the wrong reason.

03

Paired standoff, cropped after

Perspective is set by distance, so shoot the same build a second time from twice as far and crop back in post. That is real compression — the near edge stops looming — and it needs no mod change at all, because we already shoot 4K and deliver at 1600 px.

Down to a 30° effective field of view the crop is free: 1,615 px wide against a 1,600 px render. The limit is not resolution but Valheim’s 120 m haze cap — 827 of 1,007 builds can be shot from 2× and still fit under it. Only 23 are already pinned against it. derived-frames.json already carries crops of accepted captures, so the machinery exists.

The one targeting signal with a reason behind it

Every targeting rule tried so far has been a correlation that measured zero. This one is a mechanism, and you can state why it is true before measuring it: a twilight frame is worth taking in proportion to how much light the builder placed.

We cannot compute it yet. The fire vocabulary was written from Valheim's craftable build menu, and this world is built from the prefab table — so the most-placed light sources in it are invisible to the scan, exactly as the Dvergr chairs were invisible to the seat vocabulary:

Roughly 200,000 light sources the interior scan does not know exist. Fix that, get a light count per build, and the twilight pass becomes targeted instead of a blanket re-shoot — which also makes it far cheaper, since the right 30 builds are worth more than any 30.

It needs hand-auditing rather than a bulk add: a pattern sweep for “fire” also matches DvergerMageFire, which is a creature effect, and would happily swallow anything with torch in its name.
