# Community stewardship and commercial use

The project wants its work used. Healthy communities require people to do
uncelebrated operational work, and those stewards should be able to earn a
modest return without hiring a lawyer.

The boundary is extraction, not success. A community that keeps players
happy, publishes its deployed improvements, uses transparent pricing, and
creates opportunities for others is different from a large platform or
cash-driven organization repackaging the work as its own.

## Automatic Community Steward grant

The controlling terms are in [`LICENSE`](LICENSE). In summary, no separate
agreement or royalty is required when:

- the operator is an eligible individual, volunteer group, or small
  organization independent of a Large Organization;
- each deployment has no more than 100 Active Members;
- aggregate Community Revenue across commonly controlled deployments is no
  more than USD 25,000 during any rolling 12-month period;
- the complete source and reproduction material for the exact deployed
  version remains publicly available;
- prices and paid benefits are disclosed clearly;
- player personal data is not sold or licensed; and
- license, source-offer, attribution, and unofficial-project notices remain
  visible.

Qualifying stewards may charge for access, subscriptions, sponsorship,
advertising, items, services, or other benefits and may keep the resulting
profit. The full definitions and conditions in `LICENSE` control.

## When a separate agreement is needed

Contact `licensing@djcdevelopment.com` before production use if any of these
apply:

- the operator or a controlling organization is publicly traded, has more
  than USD 1,000,000 in annual gross revenue, or averages more than 10
  full-time-equivalent workers;
- a deployment exceeds 100 Active Members;
- aggregate Community Revenue exceeds USD 25,000 in a rolling 12-month period;
- deployed modifications will not be published;
- the project will be white-labeled, resold, or offered as a platform or
  managed service; or
- the use does not otherwise satisfy the automatic grant.

## The easy commercial path

The standard negotiation starts at 8.5% of Net Receipts attributable to the
licensed deployment. It is a starting point, not a take-it-or-leave-it demand.
Ask for a lower percentage or a waiver and explain the community value being
created.

The current working definition of Net Receipts is cash actually received,
less refunds, chargebacks, sales and use taxes, payment-processing and
marketplace fees, and direct infrastructure expenses attributable to the
deployment. A signed agreement will contain the controlling definition.

Reasons to reduce or waive the percentage include:

- publishing useful improvements or contributing them upstream;
- accessible pricing and demonstrated player benefit;
- enabling players to contribute valuable hardware or local capacity;
- nonprofit or public-benefit operation;
- strong documentation, support, or tooling that helps other communities; and
- low support burden on the project.

Closed modifications, white-label resale, large platforms, publishers, cloud
providers, AI companies, and publicly traded organizations require separate
review.

## What to send

A commercial request can begin with one email containing:

1. operator or organization name;
2. components and version to be used;
3. expected active members;
4. revenue model and expected annual receipts;
5. whether deployed source will remain public;
6. support requested;
7. benefits or contributions offered to the community; and
8. the percentage or waiver requested.

The goal is a short, plain-language agreement proportional to the use.

This page explains current policy. It is not itself a license, quote, or
binding commercial offer. Only `LICENSE` or a signed written agreement grants
production rights.

No project agreement grants rights in Valheim, Unity, Steam, or other
third-party software, content, or trademarks.
