# Transparent stewardship

Baseline exists to be used. Its first value is what it lets a community build;
its second is the visible record of how it was built. Wider use demonstrates
the depth of the work, tests human-and-AI collaboration in different creative
settings, and creates opportunities to make other creators more capable
without breaking their creative continuity.

The project therefore starts from abundance:

- publish source, methods, schemas, decisions, and reproducible evidence;
- let healthy small communities sustain themselves and earn a modest return;
- make improvements useful to the next community;
- treat player-owned hardware and volunteer labor with respect; and
- reserve negotiation for extraction, institutional scale, or exceptional
  risk rather than ordinary community success.

The license supplies the legal boundary. This document supplies a reference
operating framework. It is not a privacy notice, a commercial agreement, or a
substitute for the laws and platform terms that apply to a deployment.

## Public evidence, private people

The default is to make the system understandable without making a person
observable.

Public by default:

- source, protocols, schemas, build material, and configuration examples with
  secrets removed;
- measurement definitions, collection intervals, aggregation rules, known
  limitations, and software versions;
- aggregate operational and community-health trends that pass a release
  review; and
- delayed summaries of incidents, corrections, and lessons learned when
  disclosure will not create a security or privacy risk.

Private or community-controlled by default:

- account and platform identifiers, IP addresses, device identifiers, and
  contact details;
- precise event times, locations, social graphs, chat, moderation records,
  support conversations, and security logs;
- world saves, player profiles, inventories, creative work, and unpublished
  configuration;
- raw per-player or per-device telemetry; and
- information about a person's home network, contributed hardware, energy
  use, or availability.

Pseudonyms are not automatically anonymous. If the operator can reconnect a
record to a person, the record is still treated as personal data.

## Three visibility modes

Every deployable data feature should expose a clear mode and document its
default:

1. **Local/private** — data stays with the participant or community except
   where transfer is necessary to deliver the requested service.
2. **Aggregate sharing** — approved statistics may contribute to delayed
   cross-community trends, without publishing row-level records.
3. **Public contribution** — a participant or community deliberately
   contributes a named dataset, artifact, or case study under stated terms.

Silence is not consent to the third mode. Essential operational collection
must be separated from optional research or ecosystem reporting wherever
practical.

## Provisional aggregate-release profile

Until a deployment publishes a stricter profile, the reference starting point
for public trends is:

- wait at least 30 days after the end of the measured period;
- include at least 10 contributing participants or independently operated
  communities in every reported cohort;
- use ranges, rounding, or time buckets instead of exact rare values;
- suppress small cells, outliers, and combinations that could single someone
  out;
- do not publish raw identifiers or stable public pseudonyms;
- test whether the release can be joined with public or retained private
  information to identify a person; and
- publish the metric definition, date range, population, transformations, and
  important limitations with the result.

Ten participants and 30 days are floors, not a claim that data is anonymous.
Sensitive or highly distinctive activity may require a larger cohort, a
longer delay, additional privacy techniques, or no release at all. Communities
may select stricter thresholds or disable aggregate sharing.

A release reviewer records the purpose, source fields, retention period,
cohort rule, delay, transformations, re-identification risks, and approval.
The reusable review worksheet is maintained with the project's private
operating materials.

## Participant and community controls

An adopting deployment should make it easy to:

- learn what is collected, why, where it goes, and how long it is retained;
- opt out of nonessential collection and ecosystem reporting;
- export community-owned configuration, worlds, and creative artifacts in a
  usable format;
- request access, correction, or deletion when applicable;
- revoke contributed-hardware participation and credentials promptly;
- learn which operators and service providers can access private data; and
- report a privacy, security, measurement, or stewardship concern without
  retaliation.

An operator must still publish a deployment-specific privacy notice and
determine its own legal obligations, including rules that apply to children
or to particular jurisdictions.

## Independent review

A participant, community steward, commercial operator, or project maintainer
may request review when a material concern cannot be resolved directly.

The reference path is intentionally short:

1. state the concern, desired outcome, urgency, and evidence that can be
   shared safely;
2. give the responsible party a reasonable opportunity to respond and cure;
3. agree on a neutral reviewer, narrow questions, relevant records, schedule,
   confidentiality protections, and cost allocation;
4. let the reviewer inspect only what is reasonably necessary; and
5. publish a concise finding or trend-level summary when safe, while
   withholding personal data, secrets, exploit details, and privileged
   material.

The neutral may be a technically qualified community member, accountant,
security professional, mediator, or lawyer, depending on the question. The
review is not an excuse for broad surveillance or discovery. A person should
not have to publish private evidence to raise a concern, and inability to fund
a review should not prevent a good-faith concern from being heard.

The project keeps a private request form, scope-and-confidentiality template,
evidence index, decision record, and follow-up checklist so a review can begin
without inventing a process during a conflict. Those templates require
qualified review before they are used as a binding agreement.

## Reference foundations

This profile is informed by the
[NIST Privacy Framework](https://www.nist.gov/privacy-framework), including
its focus on roles across a data-processing ecosystem, and the UK Information
Commissioner's
[anonymisation guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/data-sharing/anonymisation/).
Those resources are guidance, not a declaration that this project or any
deployment complies with a particular law.

This framework should evolve in public as deployments reveal better ways to
protect people, preserve useful evidence, and help creative communities learn
from one another.
